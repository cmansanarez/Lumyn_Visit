For five days in early June, Lumyn: Constellation of Self invited visitors to pause and reflect on quiet questions — of identity, memory, kindness, resilience, joy, and queer becoming. Each reflection was offered anonymously, and each one became something more than an answer.

  • a poem

No participant was ever named or identified. Every response entered the room only as light. What follows is the memory of what gathered here.

Five Days in June

Visitors of many ages found their way to the installation. Families wandered in together. Couples sat side by side at the reflection station. Artists lingered over the projections. Children asked what the stars meant and then answered their own question. Longtime friends compared the poems they had each received, and members of the LGBTQ+ community returned across multiple days to watch the constellation grow.

Pride is joyful and loud by design. Lumyn became something adjacent to that — a quiet room inside the celebration, where people stepped out of the noise for a few minutes to sit with a question. Some typed a reflection and stayed to watch it become a poem. Others simply stood in the light. Most left slowly.

By the end of each day, the sky above the gallery held everything that had been offered to it. By each morning, it had let go, and began again.

From the Artist

Lumyn began as an exploration — of queer identity, of reflection, of what generative systems might hold if they were asked to hold something tender. I didn't know what it would become once it left my studio and entered a room full of strangers.

What it became was quieter and larger than I expected. Watching people pause in front of the projections — breathe, smile, cry, laugh, sit in silence together — became one of the most meaningful parts of this project for me. I made a system that writes poems, but the visitors made it mean something. Every reflection someone trusted the installation with was a small act of courage, and I felt the weight of that every day.

Thank you to Fort Collins Pride, to the Center for Creativity, and most of all to every person who gave a piece of their light to this constellation. It existed because you did.

— Cam Mansanarez, Noir Mak

How the Light Was Kept

Lumyn was built to hold reflections without holding on to the people who offered them.

  • Every reflection was anonymous. No names were requested, and none were kept.
  • Motion in the space was sensed in real time and released in real time — used only to let the constellation respond to presence.
  • No video was stored. No facial recognition was used.
  • Reflections were transformed into poetry, but no poem was ever publicly attributed to anyone.
  • The constellation reset every day. Each morning, the sky began empty.
